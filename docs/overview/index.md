# Research Program

**Forensic Readiness Software Engineering (FRSE)** investigates what makes evolving software systems forensically ready and how this readiness can be established, evaluated, and improved within real software engineering processes.

## Research premise

Forensic readiness is more than collecting logs after an incident. It concerns the design, maintenance, governance, accessibility, integrity, traceability, provenance, and interpretability of potentially relevant evidence before investigations are needed.

FRSE connects these concerns to concrete software artifacts, development decisions, repository history, and engineering practices. Its initial evidence base is a completed multivocal research synthesis, developed into the **FREM conceptual model**.

## Research goals

1. **Conceptualization:** establish a transparent, traceable foundation of forensic readiness in software engineering.
2. **Characterization:** investigate whether and how software artifacts and changes provide evidence supporting forensic readiness.
3. **Operationalization:** explore indicators, assessments, and analysis methods grounded in validated evidence.
4. **Improvement:** examine how engineering decisions and practices might close evidence gaps and support trustworthy investigations.

## Research organization

The program is organized into four [work packages](../work-packages/), multiple studies, and distinct outputs. Research activities and publications do not share a single status field because research completion, publication, and public release are separate events.
